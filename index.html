    Kind

    PlanetHex

    PlanetHex generates a planet from a seed and shows it two ways at once: as a flat hex map on the unfolded icosahedral net, and as a globe. Both read the same surface, so a coastline on one is the coastline on the other.

    Nothing about the surface is stored. A planet is its seed, its profile, and whatever you have written on it, and the ground is rebuilt from those every time it is opened.

    The panels

    Left
    Everything you can change, and nothing else.
    Middle
    The flat map, with the worked-out figures for the world beneath it.
    Right, top
    The globe, drawn at the size and the axial tilt the profile gives it.
    Right, bottom
    Local detail: the hex you are pointing at and the ground around it, drawn far finer than the map can resolve. This is not a magnified copy of the map — it reads the surface several levels deeper.

    Making a world

    New starts a fresh planet with a new seed, a rolled profile, and a starport placed on coastal land near its equator.

    The seed says which world this is. The same seed always gives the same ground. The UWP says what kind of world it is: Roll takes a new one from Traveller's world creation rules, or type over any digit. Gravity sets how tall the ground stands, air and water weather it, hydrographics decides how much of it is underwater, and axial tilt and pressure size the ice caps.

    Detail chooses how finely the world is drawn: 362, 1442, 5762, or 23042 hexes. It does not change the world. A coarser setting draws fewer hexes of the same planet, so the slider is safe to move and safe to move back.

    The World figures — tilt, day, orbit, temperature — arrive rolled and are yours to overrule. One you have typed over is outlined in gold, and reset beside the heading hands them all back to the roll.

    The pointer

    Map
    Click a hex to select it. Drag to pan, wheel to zoom. Whatever you point at is what the local panel draws.
    Globe
    Drag to turn it, wheel to zoom, click a hex to select it. It picks its own spin back up once you have been still for a moment.
    Local detail
    Click one of the fine hexes to put a point of interest on it, or to open the one already there.

    Points of interest

    A starport is a place on the world; a comment is a note about one. Each has a name and as much prose as you care to write, and both are marked on the map and the globe in their own colour. They are placed in the local panel, because the map's hexes are hundreds of miles across and far too large to say where anything is.

    One to a point. Clicking an empty point offers Move Here, which brings an existing one over with its name and its notes intact. The left panel lists what is on the world; choosing one goes to it.

    Hex names

    A hex is named F07R33C08 — face, row, and column, counted on the finest lattice, so the name is the same whatever the Detail slider is set to. A point finer than the map draws carries the depth after a slash. Write one down and it will still mean the same ground tomorrow.

    Saving

    A save is a folder holding one planet: its JSON, and the flat map as a picture at each of the four detail levels. The first Save asks where the planet goes and later ones do not ask again. Load opens the JSON. Nothing is sent anywhere — no server is involved and no planet leaves this machine.

    Drawing four maps takes a few seconds, most of it the finest one and its 23,042 hexes. The panel says what it is doing while it works.

    unsaved appears at the top of the left panel when the open planet has changes that are not written down, and anything that would discard them asks first.

    Saving and loading need a Chromium browser — Chrome or Edge — or the desktop build, which carries one. Firefox and Safari can draw a world but cannot write one.
